01 — The 3 Tasks to Master

The written expression test, break down.

60 Minutes, 3 Tasks, a precise marking scheme. Here's what the examiner expects at each stage.

01 15–20 min · 60–80 words

Short Message

Email · Letter · Message

You are writing a message to describe, recount or explain a situation to a specific recipient.

  • Formal or semi-formal register depending on the context.
  • Strict adherence to instructions (recipient, purpose).
  • Appropriate address and closing formula.
“Write a message to your roommate asking them to pay more attention to cleaning, while remaining polite.”
02 20–25 min · 120–150 words

Blog Post

Article · Forum · Opinion piece

You develop your personal opinion on a given subject by providing arguments and examples.

  • Current register, adapted for the web.
  • 2 to 3 structured and supported arguments.
  • Introduction + development + conclusion.
“Should we limit teenagers' use of social media? Share your opinion on a youth blog.”
03 25–30 min · 120–180 words

Comparative article

Summary + position statement

You summarize two opposing viewpoints and then you defend your own with arguments.

  • Part 1: 40–60 words — neutral summary.
  • Part 2: 80–120 words — your opinion.
  • Do not copy the source documents.
“Two opposing views on teleworking. Summarize them and then take a reasoned position.”

04 — Things to Do / Things to Avoid

The 10 rules that can make or break your grade.

What our markers spot first in NCLC 9 papers — and the errors that sink NCLC 6 papers.

To Do

Winning reflexes.

  • Quick outline in draft form before writing (2 min max).
  • Respect the required length — cheating on the counter will result in a penalty.
  • Vary the logical connectors (first, furthermore, however, thus…).
  • Register adapted to the recipient — no “hello” to your manager.
  • Take a clear position in Task 3. No “I don't know”.
To Avoid

Costly mistakes.

  • Sentences that are too long and complex — you're making a lot of mistakes.
  • Copy the source documents in Task 3 (zero points).
  • Forget the address or closing formula in Task 1
  • Mix formal and informal registers in the same copy.
  • Rushing the proofreading — leaving 5 minutes at the end is non-negotiable.
